Fitting that in October I succumb to the family curse.

![zine](/images/glutes/werewolfbarmitzvah.gif)

<!--more-->

### spooky scary

I've been drafting this for a while in my head so we'll see how much of it lives to see the light of day. While some tests are still pending re: celiac proper, it appears I can no longer stand gluten (that bastard), one of my all time favorite foods. Which is, what we in the business like to call, a bummer. Given my family history (family members on both sides with food allergies) it's probably a miracle I've lasted as long as I have, scarfing down pizza and beer. 

The real frustrating thing about it is how many things just contain gluten for no good reason. Obviously yes, I know I can't dive into the garlic bread and wheat beers now, but even things like Twizzler Nibs have gluten. The fuck is up with that? Or Rice Krispies. A life of checking labels.

This has largely been my life for the past few weeks, and while it feels like it came to the tipping point very suddenly, having carved the ol' glutanious grains out of my diet also appears to have remedied symptoms that were more low-lying but noticiable for a long time. Likely my sensativity/allergy had been slowly ramping up until I suddenly was very aware of it.

So I've been trawling gluten free food aisles for a bit now. Seeing what there is. Trying the options to see what I like. 

Turns out, I don't like much of it. 

A quick rundown of GF food alternatives I've tried so far:
* Gluten Free beer: mostly fine, just tastes like an over-hopped IPA. Expensive as shit though. $15 for 4 cans
* Red lentil pasta: I wanted to like this, mostly because I wanted it to be like a red-pepper based pasta from a local joint, but it was too chewy and gritty
* GF bread: mostly fine, if dry as hell. Needs a heartly slathering of mayo or mustard. Alright as toast
* GF crackers, various: Medium to poor. Nuts are not meant to be turned into crackers. Sits like a brick in your stomach
* GF snack cakes: Dry, saved only by the chocolate
* GF cookies: Dry, sad, not saved by the chocolate
* GF noodles: Mostly fine. Need to find a decent sauce since most Americanized Asian style sauces have gluten
* French fry crust pizza: maybe a 5-6/10, but would have been 8+ if they just put some goddamn pizza sauce on it. The adherence to "things you would put on french fries" really hampers it.

It also sends you down a wild rabbit hole of looking for "non-replacement" foods that are GF - changing to corn tortillas instead of using chickpea flour tortillas, for example. Turns out a lot of junk food is still GF. Chips and Fuity Pebbles are a constant snack now.

Which brings me to what appear to be the two schools of thought on this - finding GF replacements for foods you can no longer eat, or just pretending those foods don't exist anymore and doing without. Those are how my dad and my mom handle it, respectively. As I become more and more disillusioned with the GF options, I'm starting to sway more to the "just forget they exist" camp. The GF options don't spark joy, so what's the point?

Which is really the bummer of this thing - it's like suddenly being very aware that I can't eat X or Y anymore. One day it hit me: no more cinnamon rolls. No more pizza. Food that brings me joy and that I like sharing with people I love. I love the cook, and this really puts a shit on the whole thing. It's either a life of two meals or dragging my family down into the GF mire that I will now be living in. 

Additionally, having gone roughly 40 years pro-glute, it sucks because I know what a good pizza tastes like. I know what good, fluffy pancakes taste like. So now knowing I can't have that anymore - I don't want to presume I know the experience of people who have never been able to have gluten but, I feel like with never knowing, it's easier to give something up. Instead I'm trying to navigate what I *can* eat without getting nauseated and breaking out in a rash and feeling my throat get thick and it sucks. I'm steps way from giving up and moving to a gruel-as-fuel diet.

Honestly, this whole thing is depressing.
