The problem with the "*Die Hard* is a Christmas movie" argument is it ignores the dichotomy of "secular Christmas-as-a-winter-holiday" idea vs "religious Christmas-as-magic" bullshit.

Think of it this way: 

*It's A Wonderful Life* is a Christmas movie because it involves Christianity magical bullshit where a literal Christian angel shows up to shut down an individual's personal agency. (Now if *that* isn't indicative of religion with a capital R, I don't know what is.)

*Enemy of the State* is a movie that just HAPPENS during Christmas. We see Christmas decorations, a plot point involves Christmas shopping. But replace that plot point with any other gift giving occasion and the plot still works. There is no magical Christianity supernatural religious power bullshit at play. It could have equally happened during Valentine's Day, Hanukkah, Arbor Day, you name it. The holiday in the plot is simply a means to an end.

*Die Hard*, *Home Alone*, *A Christmas Story*, *Christmas Vacation*, that one movie where all the couples break up and it has every famous actor ever but for some reason everyone still likes it, *Catch Me If You Can*, all those "classic Christmas movies" are not actually Christmas movies. They just happen to take place during that time of year. 

There's very few "actual Christmas movies" because movie makers don't want to go hard in the paint on capital R religion and cut out demographics. A lot of what we consider "Christmas movies" could easily transition over to Hanukkah or Kwanzaa or Winter Solstice or any other "general holiday occurring during snow where you maybe gift someone something" (see above).

Aside:

Santa movies are a weird kinda grey area, but I tend to put those in with religious-Christmas-as-magical-bullshit (RCAMB) for the following reasons: 

Santa may not have started as such but has kinda become a secularization of RCAMB - he was/is a "saint," he's specific to the Xmas holiday, BUT he doesn't actually appear to interact with or gain powers from YHWH in the same way that the angel in *Wonderful Life* did. 

HOWEVER, most Santa movies are steeped in magical bullshit, no matter how they try to cut loose from Christianity. Everything about Santa is magical bullshit. They flying reindeer. Keeping a list. Flying around the world in one night. Elves. The moral panopticon. (There are some non-Magical Santa movies like *Bad Santa* that would again fall back into the Not A Christmas Movie realm).

Magicical-Santa movies tend to be the "religious middle ground" of capital R religious bullshit Christmas movies - they have *just enough* religious bullshit to force their morality down the throats of the characters and the viewers, but tend to keep all of it framed in the quasi-secular "Santa" character in order to not be percieved as full blown Christian indoctrination.

Hell, even the movie *Elf* is a RCAMB movie for this reason. Also *Miracle on 34th St*, *Santa Clause*, etc etc.

Bottom line, if there's magical bullshit, it's a Christmas movie (or moving that direction on the spectrum).

And what movie doesn't have magical capital R religious bullshit? 

That's right, *Die Hard*.

Ergo, not a Christmas movie.
