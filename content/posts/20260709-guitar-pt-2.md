# Part Two: Surgery

As I mentioned [previously](https://blog.floppy.parts/posts/20260702-guitar-hunt-pt1/) I was going to work on my existing guitar a little bit before searching out a new one. 

<!--more-->

A smiple procedure: restring, and while the strings were off, take off the pick guard to take a liitle looksie inside to see if we could figure out why the jack crackles occasionally. 

But first, we set our scene in fair Verona:

I had to decide on some operating room music. I went with *Bat Out of Hell*, a long time favorite from my childhood, and Sadao Watanabe's *Rendezvous*, which I have not listened to before. 

![Cover](/images/guitar2/1.jpg )

Quick aside about *Bat Out Of Hell* - This album is/was a favorite of my mom's, and when I was younger (6 or 7? maybe a few years older) she was making mix tapes for my sibling and I based on requests from our family CD collection. We both had personal tape players so I think my mom's idea was if we picked our own songs and had a tape we could listen to individually we wouldn't all be fighting over what CD was in the living room stereo. Anyway, she asked what song I wanted next and I wanted the title song *Bat Out of Hell*, but being a kid I didn't think I could say "Hell" in my response so it was a lot of "that song" (pointing at the title) or "the first song" as we played a bad rendition of "Who's On First" (As written by Jim Steinman and performed by Meat Loaf). 

I also needed to be properly hydrated while working:

![Cover](/images/guitar2/2.jpg )

So we can begin.

### Like a surgeon

![Cover](/images/guitar2/3.jpg )

Got my tools, got my strings, I 3d printed a neck rest, I'm ready to go.

Unwinding and cutting the strings felt a little ... wrong? I don't know. Like I was at the edge of "what I knew about guitar maintenance" and "what the hell am I doing?" But at that point it was no going back, no chickening out. I broke it, so now I had to buy it (with blood, sweat, and tears).

 ![Cover](/images/guitar2/4.jpg )

In doing so, I didn't realize my wraparound bridge would pop right off, but there it did. However, that does give me a better view of the thing, and a couple of possible parts numbers or serial numbers. Looking online, recasts of these are in the $180 range.

### Underneath it all

Unscrewing the pick guard and opening it up gives me a couple more serial numbers, and the model number (which we already knew). It's nice to know Good Ol' Number 26 inspected this unit.

![Cover](/images/guitar2/9.jpg )
![Cover](/images/guitar2/10.jpg )
![Cover](/images/guitar2/11.jpg )
![Cover](/images/guitar2/12.jpg )

I didn't really get a good photo of the jack itself, but looking at it while the guard was open I didn't see anything necessarily bent out of position. I did use a wrench to tighten the nut around the jack more than I could by hand though, in case that was the issue.

It was at about this point I switched over to the Sadao Watanabe tape. It was quite enjoyable.

### Finger lickin'

With one issue (hopefully) taken care of, I moved on to cleaning the guitar the best I could before restringing it. The body and guard had some light dust that was easy to clean up, but I also took a crack at the fretboard which was a little gunky. A combination of scraping off finger gunk and some microcloth cleaning with water and alcohol seemed to improve it, at least. (This is the before photo)

![Cover](/images/guitar2/13.jpg )

And really, stringing it back up took the longest time. I wasn't entirelly sure what I was doing so I was tightening slowly and then tuning slowly - turns out I was told after the fact that once you have the strings on you can just give them a heave and pull them out perpendicularly to speed up the stretching process. I didn't know that at the time and instead slowly tuned them (as it felt like every knob twist the strings were going to snap) and then by the time I got done tuning all 6 strings I could start over because they'd stretched some more.

All in all though, mission accomplished. New strings on, it stays in tune, and it plays. Now on the hunt for a guitar to really get weird with.
